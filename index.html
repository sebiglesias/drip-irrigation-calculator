Frequently Asked Questions

What is the maximum flow rate of 1/2-inch drip irrigation tubing?
Standard 1/2-inch poly drip tubing (.600 ID x .700 OD) has a maximum capacity of 200 to 220 Gallons Per Hour (GPH) or 3.6 GPM. If your emitters total more than 220 GPH, water pressure will drop drastically at the end of the line. Split your garden into two independent zones using an automatic dual-zone timer.
Why is a 25 PSI pressure regulator mandatory on drip systems?
Household outdoor hose spigots deliver municipal water pressure between 50 and 80 PSI. Drip fittings and micro-tubing are designed for low pressure (20 to 30 PSI). Without a 25 PSI regulator, fittings will blow apart and emitters will spray water like geysers.
What is a Pressure Compensating (PC) emitter?
Pressure compensating emitters have an internal flexible silicone diaphragm that regulates flow. Whether the emitter is located at the beginning of the hose at 30 PSI or 100 feet downhill at 18 PSI, it delivers the exact same rate of water (e.g., 1.0 GPH) to every single plant.
